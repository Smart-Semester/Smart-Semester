# Product Requirements Document: Smart Semester

**Team:** GDG University Club — 4-person team
**Status:** Draft v1.0
**Last Updated:** September 11, 2026
**Demo Day:** December 8, 2026

---

## 1. Overview

Smart Semester is a registration-planning helper for university students. Given a student's degree program and their planned courses for an upcoming semester, the app checks prerequisite/corequisite eligibility and surfaces curated information and resources for each planned course — including course summaries (workload, topics, projects, past syllabi insights) and external prep resources (LeetCode, HackerRank, Hello Interview, YouTube playlists).

The goal is to reduce the friction and guesswork of semester planning by giving students a single view of "here's what these classes actually involve, whether you're eligible to take them, and how to prepare."

## 2. Problem Statement

Students planning a semester today have to piece together information from multiple disconnected sources: degree audit tools (which only tell you what's missing, not what a course is *like*), scattered old syllabi, Reddit/Discord threads, and ad hoc resource lists shared by upperclassmen. There's no single tool that combines eligibility checking with practical, content-level insight into what a course will demand and how to prepare for it.

The team has personally experienced this gap while planning their own semesters, which motivates both the project and its scope.

## 3. Goals

- Give students a fast way to sanity-check a planned course load against prerequisite/corequisite requirements.
- Give students real insight into what a course involves before they commit to it (workload, content, project types).
- Curate high-quality external prep resources per course so students can start preparing early.
- Provide a genuinely usable tool the team (and ideally other students) would use for their own registration.
- Use the project as a learning vehicle for the full modern web/cloud stack: Next.js, FastAPI, Postgres, Docker, CI/CD, and GCP.

### Differentiation

Smart Semester is **not** a degree audit tool. TXST's existing degree audit / "what-if" tool tells students what requirements they're missing and what courses they still need. Smart Semester assumes the student already knows (or has picked) their planned courses and instead focuses on:
- Whether those specific planned courses are currently eligible (prereq/coreq check), and
- What those courses actually involve and how to prepare for them.

The two tools are complementary, not competing — Smart Semester picks up where a degree audit leaves off.

## 4. Target Users

University students who are actively planning their course schedule for an upcoming semester, particularly:
- Students trying to gauge course difficulty/workload before registering
- Students who want to start prepping for a technical course (e.g., DSA-heavy courses) before the semester starts
- Students who want a quick prereq/coreq sanity check without digging through a full degree audit

**For MVP, this is scoped specifically to Computer Science majors** (see Section 5), since degree/prereq data acquisition for every major on campus isn't feasible for the team's timeline and bandwidth.

## 5. Scope

**MVP scope note:** Smart Semester will initially support only the **Computer Science major** (current catalog year, expanding to historical years as time allows). Full multi-major support is not feasible given the manual data-entry burden for prose-formatted degree requirements (see Section 6), so CS was chosen as the initial focus. Expanding to additional majors is a post-MVP consideration (see Section 5.2).

### 5.1 MVP Features (v1 — required for Demo Day, Dec 8)

| # | Feature | Description |
|---|---------|-------------|
| 1 | **Course Summaries** | Per-course page/card showing what the course covers, typical workload, project types, and takeaways aggregated from past syllabi. Summaries are generated by calling an LLM and caching the response per course in Postgres; the LLM is only called for courses without an existing cached summary, so each course is summarized once and reused thereafter. |
| 2 | **Curated Resources per Course** | Hand-picked/aggregated links per course: LeetCode sets, HackerRank tracks, Hello Interview content, relevant YouTube playlists. |
| 3 | **Schedule Builder** | Students select their planned courses for a semester and see a unified view with summaries + resources for all selected courses at once. |
| 4 | **Prereq/Coreq Warning** | Given the student's degree + completed/planned courses, flag any planned course where prerequisite or corequisite requirements aren't met. |

### 5.2 Post-MVP / Backlog (Nice-to-Have, not required for Dec 8)

- Personalized resource recommendations based on student performance/history
- Instructor-specific syllabus/workload variance (not just course-level)
- Community-submitted course reviews or resource suggestions
- Multi-semester planning (not just one semester at a time)
- Support for additional majors beyond Computer Science
- Integration with university registration systems (if APIs are available)
- Mobile-friendly / native app packaging

### 5.3 Out of Scope

- Full degree audit / "what's left to graduate" functionality (this is the TXST tool's job)
- Actual course registration/enrollment (Smart Semester is informational/planning only, not transactional)
- Payment or paid resource curation — no budget is allocated to this project

## 6. Constraints

- **Budget:** $0. No paid services, APIs, or hosting beyond free tiers.
- **Cloud provider:** Must use Google Cloud Platform (fixed requirement).
- **Team size/bandwidth:** 4 students balancing this alongside coursework, on a fixed 14-week program timeline with graded weekly milestones.
- **Data sourcing (degree requirements & prereqs):** Degree and course requirement data will be sourced by scraping TXST's public undergraduate catalog sites — the [current catalog](https://mycatalog.txstate.edu/undergraduate/majors/) and [previous years' catalogs](https://www.txst.edu/curriculumservices/catalogs/undergraduate/catalogs.html). Scraping is confirmed permitted per the sites' robots.txt. Both sites use plain HTML in a consistent format across majors. Where requirements are presented in tables, they will be scraped directly. Where requirements are presented as prose (e.g., "Students must complete 46 hours of Computer Science courses..."), they will be manually entered by the team, since these are lower-volume and harder to parse reliably. **Given this manual-entry burden, MVP scope is limited to the Computer Science major only** — supporting every major's requirements was judged infeasible for the team's timeline. Historical catalogs allow the tool to support students on older catalog years, not just the current one, for the CS major.
- **Data sourcing (course summaries):** Rather than sourcing past syllabi directly, course summaries will be generated on-demand by an LLM call and cached per course (see Section 5.1). This avoids needing a syllabus dataset up front and keeps ongoing LLM usage low after the initial cache is populated.

## 7. Tech Stack

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Frontend | Next.js (TypeScript) |
| Backend | FastAPI (Python) |
| Database | PostgreSQL |
| Containerization | Docker |
| Cloud | Google Cloud Platform |
| CI/CD | TBD tooling, but required to be functional by Week 6 (Oct 6) |
| Course Summary Generation | LLM API call per course, response cached in Postgres (call skipped if a cached summary already exists) |
| Degree/Prereq Data Ingest | Scraper against TXST's public undergraduate catalog sites (current + historical), for table-formatted requirements; manual entry for prose-formatted requirements |

**Architecture decision (monolith vs. microservices):** The team has chosen to **start with microservices from the outset**, split roughly along the four ownership areas in Section 8 (catalog/ingest, resources/summaries, frontend/planner, infra/gateway). The primary drivers are enabling each team member to work independently on their own service without blocking on others, and gaining direct experience with microservices architecture and orchestration.

## 8. Team Roles

Given the microservices decision (Section 7), each role below maps to ownership of a distinct service, allowing members to develop and deploy independently.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Catalog Data & Ingest | Sourcing and structuring degree/course/prereq data; building the ingestion pipeline into Postgres. |
| Resources & Summaries | Curating and structuring per-course resources (LeetCode, HackerRank, Hello Interview, YouTube) and handling generating and caching course generated summaries. |
| Frontend & Planner | Building the Next.js frontend, including the schedule builder UI and course/resource views. |
| Infra, CI/CD, Gateway & Cloud Deployment | Docker setup, CI/CD pipeline, GCP infrastructure (IAM, networking, secrets, container registry), API gateway, deployment. |

## 9. Success Metrics

Since this is a program-graded project rather than a metrics-driven product launch, success will be measured primarily by:
- Meeting each weekly program milestone (see Section 10) on schedule
- A working, deployed end-to-end demo by Dec 8 covering all four MVP features
- Passing the Week 12 Industry Architecture Review with credible architecture, code quality, deployment, and security practices
- Secondary/stretch: the team (or other students) actually using the tool for their own Spring registration planning

## 12. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Manual entry of prose-formatted degree requirements is time-intensive | Scope limited to the Computer Science major only for MVP; start ingest/data work early (Week 3–4) with the current catalog year first, expanding to historical years only as time allows. |
| Catalog site structure changes or scraping breaks | Keep the scraper isolated from the rest of the ingest pipeline so it can be re-run or patched without affecting cached data already in Postgres. |
| LLM-generated course summaries could be inaccurate or low quality, or costs could grow if the cache isn't hit | Cache aggressively per course (call the LLM once per course, not per request); review a sample of generated summaries for quality before relying on them for the demo; choose a provider with a free tier. |
| Zero budget limits use of paid APIs/data providers | Rely on GCP free tier, public catalog data, an LLM free tier, and manually curated resource lists. |
| Starting with microservices adds upfront complexity (multiple deployments, service-to-service contracts, more GCP resources to manage) on a tight $0-budget timeline | Keep service boundaries small and stable, define API contracts early (by Week 3 Architecture Proposal) to avoid renegotiating them mid-project; keep each service's infra footprint minimal (e.g., lightweight containers) to stay within free-tier limits; lean on the CI/CD (Week 6) and Cloud Foundation (Week 7) milestones to catch integration issues early rather than at the end. |
| Four concurrent workstreams (data, resources, frontend, infra) may drift out of sync | Bi-weekly check-ins + shared repo branching strategy defined at Week 3 to keep integration continuous rather than a big-bang merge. |
